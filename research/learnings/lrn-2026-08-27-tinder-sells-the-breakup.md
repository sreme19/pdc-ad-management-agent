## Claim

Tinder India's biggest content bet is the breakup rather than the match, and it is their highest-engagement material

## Evidence

- (2026-08-27) Four distinct Tinder India campaigns observed are all about ending a relationship: Move On Salon with Sapna Bhavnani (post-breakup haircuts, 7,346 likes on the ft. Urooj episode — the highest single number across every brand post captured in this session), the Tinder x Dunkin Move On Box counter-programmed against Valentine's week 7-14 Feb, #DumpYourExCess 2.0 which pays a month of Tinder Plus for blocking your ex, and Axe Your Ex. They are buying attention at the moment of exit from someone else's relationship rather than at the moment of wanting a new one. Riteangle's rules/creative-style.md emotional threads are all entry-moment: the desolate man, the flooded woman, the camera as judge. Caveat: engagement is not conversion, and a breakup hook suits a swipe app's re-acquisition loop in ways it may not suit a matchmaking product.
- (2026-08-27) **supported**: Move On Salon is confirmed as at least a three-episode series (ft. Akriti Negi, Urooj Ashfaq, Prish), and Tinder India's own 11 Feb 2026 press release describes it as a street-side makeover pop-up in Mumbai where participants traded real breakup stories for a haircut. So the highest-engagement asset in the observed set (7,346 likes) is not a studio production but a filmed IRL activation — the breakup bet is bigger and more operationally committed than a content series. See note-2026-08-27-tinder-move-on-salon-and-nykaa.
- (2026-09-07) **contradicted** (from rec-2026-08-27-moveon-w2530-meta): RECONCILED 2026-09-07 against the live account; the ledger had carried this as 'live' for 10 days after it stopped. Ad set 6984035818681 and ad 6984036525881 are both PAUSED and the run window closed 2026-09-02. IT DID DELIVER, and well, at the top of the funnel: Rs 366.39 spend, 9,606 impressions, 606 link clicks, 465 Meta-counted landing-page views, and 611 page views recorded on OUR side for this ad set - so its url_tags worked and the traffic was tracked correctly end to end. THE VERDICT IS not-working BECAUSE OF WHAT HAPPENED NEXT: 611 tracked page views produced ZERO signups. That is well above MIN_SAMPLE=30, so this is a finding rather than a shrug - the ad bought attention on /get/w cheaply and none of it converted. Two caveats recorded rather than buried. (1) The record states Rs 1,000/day; the ad set's actual daily_budget was Rs 300/day, so this ran at under a third of its planned rate and never reached the Rs 800-1,200 band where budget.md says delivery exits learning - the CONVERSION read is solid on n=611 views, the cost-efficiency read is not. (2) Success metric as stated was landing-page views, and on that metric alone it succeeded; the verdict is against the outcome the views existed to produce.
